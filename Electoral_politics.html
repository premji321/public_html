What is the meaning of electoral politics?

Electoral politics refers to the political activities, processes, and strategies associated with conducting elections and participation in the electoral system. It involves the ways in which political parties, candidates, and voters engage with the democratic process to choose representatives, form governments, and make policy decisions.

Define Election

An election is a process where people vote to choose leaders, make decisions, or approve policies. It allows citizens to participate in selecting representatives or deciding on important issues for the community or country.

Types of Elections in India

  • General Elections: Held periodically to elect representatives, such as members of the Lok Sabha or a State Legislative Assembly.
  • Mid-term Elections: Conducted if a legislature is dissolved before completing its normal term.
  • By-elections: Held to fill a vacant seat caused by resignation, death, or disqualification of a representative.

Story of Assembly Election in Haryana

  • In 1982, Haryana was led by the Congress Party under Chief Minister Bhajan Lal.
  • Chaudhary Devi Lal led the “Nyaya Yuddh” movement and formed the Lok Dal party.
  • In the 1987 Haryana Assembly elections, the ruling Congress Party was defeated, and the Lok Dal-led alliance won a large majority.
  • This election demonstrated how voters can bring political change through elections.

Why Do We Need Elections?

  • Empower citizens to choose their representatives.
  • Ensure accountability of leaders.
  • Give legitimacy to the government.
  • Allow peaceful changes of government.
  • Provide citizens with a way to express their opinions.
  • Ensure representation of different groups and interests.

What Features Make an Election Democratic?

  • Universal Adult Suffrage: Every eligible adult citizen has the right to vote, subject to law.
  • Free and Fair Elections: Voters can choose without coercion or manipulation.
  • Regular Elections: Elections are held at constitutionally prescribed intervals.
  • Secret Ballot: A voter's choice remains confidential.
  • Political Choice: Voters have a genuine choice between candidates or parties.
  • Equal Value of Votes: Votes are counted according to the same rules.

Political Competition

Political competition refers to the contest between political parties, candidates, or groups for power and influence in government. It plays an important role in democracy, but it has both merits and demerits.

Merits of Political Competition

  • Accountability: Leaders remain answerable to voters.
  • Better Governance: Parties have an incentive to respond to public needs.
  • Strengthens Democracy: Competition provides voters with alternatives.
  • Voter Participation: Campaigns encourage people to discuss public issues.
  • Limits Concentration of Power: The possibility of losing an election can discourage misuse of power.

Demerits of Political Competition

  • Polarisation: Competition can deepen divisions in society.
  • Negative Campaigning: Candidates may attack opponents instead of discussing policies.
  • Short-term Focus: Leaders may prioritise immediate electoral gains.
  • Pressure on Representatives: Politicians may focus more on winning than on long-term public welfare.

Electoral Constituencies

An electoral constituency is a geographical area from which voters elect a representative to a legislature. For example, voters in a Lok Sabha constituency elect a Member of Parliament.

Reserved Constituencies

Reserved constituencies are constituencies in which only candidates belonging to specified constitutionally recognised groups, such as Scheduled Castes (SC) or Scheduled Tribes (ST), can contest. All eligible voters in the constituency can vote.

Why are constituencies reserved?

  • To improve representation of historically disadvantaged groups.
  • To ensure that these groups have opportunities to elect representatives from their communities.
  • To make representative institutions more inclusive.

How many Lok Sabha seats are reserved?

The number of reserved seats can change after delimitation. The figures commonly used for the current Lok Sabha are 84 seats for Scheduled Castes and 47 seats for Scheduled Tribes.

Voter's List

A voter's list, also called an electoral roll, is the official list of eligible registered voters in a constituency. Electoral rolls are prepared and updated so that eligible citizens can exercise their voting rights.

Election Campaign

An election campaign is an organised effort by candidates and political parties to explain their policies, communicate with voters and seek support before an election.

Campaign activities may include public meetings, speeches, rallies, advertisements, door-to-door visits, debates and distribution of election manifestos.

Restrictions during election campaigns

  • Model Code of Conduct: Parties and candidates are expected to follow the applicable election rules.
  • No Vote Buying: Offering money or gifts to influence votes is prohibited.
  • No Intimidation: Voters must not be threatened or forced to vote in a particular way.
  • Government Resources: Official resources must not be misused to gain an unfair electoral advantage.
  • Campaign Period: Campaigning must stop during the legally prescribed silence period.
  • Public Order: Meetings and rallies must comply with applicable rules.
  • Responsible Speech: Campaigns must comply with laws governing hate speech and other prohibited conduct.

Important Slogans Given by Political Parties

  • "Garibi Hatao": A famous slogan associated with Indira Gandhi and the Congress election campaign of 1971.
  • "Jai Jawan, Jai Kisan": A slogan given by Lal Bahadur Shastri in 1965, highlighting the importance of soldiers and farmers.
  • "Save Democracy": The defence of democracy was an important theme in opposition campaigns during the 1977 election after the Emergency.

Features that Make Elections in India Democratic

  • Universal Adult Suffrage: Eligible Indian citizens aged 18 or above can register to vote, subject to legal requirements.
  • Regular Elections: Elections are held according to constitutional and legal provisions.
  • Secret Ballot: Voters can make their choices privately.
  • Political Competition: Candidates and parties can compete under election rules.
  • Independent Election Commission: The Election Commission of India supervises elections within its constitutional jurisdiction.
  • Acceptance of Results: Democratic institutions and participants are expected to respect lawful election outcomes.

Independence of the Election Commission in India

  • Constitutional Status: Article 324 of the Constitution provides for the Election Commission of India and its powers over elections covered by that article.
  • Election Supervision: The Commission supervises, directs and controls elections within its jurisdiction.
  • Legal Safeguards: The Constitution provides special protection for the removal of the Chief Election Commissioner.
  • Enforcement: The Commission can take action under applicable election laws and rules.
  • Fairness: Its independence helps protect the election process from improper interference.

Challenges in Conducting Free and Fair Elections in India

  • Electoral Fraud and Malpractice: Attempts to influence voting through illegal means can undermine public confidence.
  • Money Power: Excessive or illegal spending can create an unfair advantage for wealthy candidates.
  • Muscle Power: Threats, intimidation and violence can prevent voters from exercising their choices freely.
  • Caste and Communal Appeals: Divisive appeals can distract from public issues and increase social tensions.
  • Inaccurate Voter Lists: Errors or omissions can prevent eligible voters from voting.
  • Misuse of Official Resources: Using government resources for partisan campaigning can create an uneven playing field.
