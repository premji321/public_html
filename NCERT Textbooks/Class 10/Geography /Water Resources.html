Water is an essential natural resource for life and development.

1. Introduction to Water Resources

−

Water is one of the most important natural resources on Earth. All living organisms require water for their survival. Human beings depend on water for drinking, agriculture, industries, transportation and several other activities.

Although approximately three-fourths of the Earth's surface is covered with water, only a small proportion of it is available as usable freshwater.

Important Concept: Water is a renewable resource because it is continuously replenished through the hydrological cycle. However, freshwater availability is limited and unevenly distributed.

Major Sources of Water

  • Rivers and streams
  • Lakes and ponds
  • Groundwater
  • Glaciers and snowfields
  • Rainwater
  • Reservoirs and tanks

Hydrological Cycle

The continuous movement of water between the oceans, atmosphere and land is known as the hydrological cycle or water cycle.

Main Processes of the Water Cycle

  1. Evaporation: Water changes into water vapour due to the heat of the Sun.
  2. Transpiration: Plants release water vapour into the atmosphere.
  3. Condensation: Water vapour cools and forms tiny water droplets and clouds.
  4. Precipitation: Water falls to the Earth's surface as rain, snow or hail.
  5. Runoff: Water flows over the land into rivers, lakes and oceans.
  6. Infiltration: Some water enters the soil and replenishes groundwater.

2. Importance of Water Resources

−

Water plays a significant role in the development of human society and the economy. It is essential for both natural ecosystems and human activities.

A. Domestic Uses

  • Drinking and cooking
  • Bathing and washing
  • Cleaning and sanitation
  • Household gardening

B. Agricultural Uses

  • Irrigation of agricultural fields
  • Growing food crops
  • Maintaining livestock
  • Supporting agricultural productivity

C. Industrial Uses

  • Manufacturing and processing
  • Cooling industrial machinery
  • Generating electricity
  • Cleaning and production processes

D. Other Uses

  • Hydroelectric power generation
  • Navigation and transportation
  • Fishing and aquaculture
  • Tourism and recreation
  • Maintaining ecological balance
Key Point: Water is necessary for human survival, economic development and the maintenance of ecosystems.

3. Water Scarcity and the Need for Water Conservation

−

Meaning of Water Scarcity

Water scarcity refers to a situation in which the availability of water is insufficient to meet the demands of people, agriculture, industries and ecosystems.

Water scarcity may occur due to insufficient rainfall, excessive use of water, pollution, population growth and unequal distribution of water resources.

Why is Water Scarcity Increasing?

  • Rapid population growth
  • Urbanisation and industrialisation
  • Increasing demand for irrigation
  • Over-exploitation of groundwater
  • Water pollution
  • Unequal distribution of water resources
  • Changing rainfall patterns and droughts

Need for Water Conservation

  1. To ensure water availability for future generations.
  2. To protect ecosystems and biodiversity.
  3. To reduce the effects of droughts.
  4. To maintain agricultural productivity.
  5. To prevent the depletion of groundwater.
  6. To support sustainable development.
Water scarcity is not always caused by the absence of rainfall. It can also result from poor management, pollution and excessive consumption.

4. Major Causes of Water Scarcity

−

1. Population Growth

As population increases, the demand for drinking water, food production, sanitation and other services also increases.

2. Intensive Irrigation

Agriculture is a major consumer of freshwater. Intensive irrigation in water-intensive crops can place heavy pressure on water resources.

3. Industrialisation

Industries require large quantities of water for manufacturing, cooling and processing. Industrial waste may also contaminate water sources.

4. Urbanisation

Growing towns and cities require more water for households, commercial establishments and infrastructure.

5. Over-exploitation of Groundwater

Excessive extraction of groundwater through tube wells and pumps lowers the groundwater table.

6. Water Pollution

Industrial effluents, domestic sewage, agricultural chemicals and other pollutants reduce the availability of safe water.

7. Unequal Distribution

Water resources are not evenly distributed across regions. Some areas receive abundant rainfall while others experience drought conditions.

5. Water Resources in India

−

India has a large network of rivers, lakes, groundwater resources and glaciers. However, water availability differs from one region to another and from one season to another.

Major River Systems of India

  • Himalayan Rivers: Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems.
  • Peninsular Rivers: Godavari, Krishna, Kaveri, Mahanadi, Narmada and Tapi.

Factors Affecting Water Availability in India

  • Monsoon rainfall and its distribution
  • Physical features and relief
  • River systems and drainage patterns
  • Groundwater availability
  • Population distribution
  • Water consumption patterns
Remember: India receives most of its annual rainfall during the monsoon season. Therefore, seasonal and regional variations in water availability are important challenges.

6. Availability of Fresh Water

−

Most of the Earth's water is saline and is found in oceans and seas. Only a small portion is freshwater.

Distribution of Earth's Water

  • Approximately 97.5% of the Earth's water is saline.
  • Approximately 2.5% is freshwater.
  • A large proportion of freshwater is stored in glaciers and ice caps or underground.
  • Only a very small amount is available in rivers and lakes for direct human use.
These are approximate global estimates. The exact percentages may vary slightly depending on the source and classification.

Freshwater Sources

  1. Surface water – rivers, lakes and reservoirs.
  2. Groundwater – water stored beneath the Earth's surface.
  3. Glaciers and snow – frozen freshwater reserves.
  4. Rainwater – precipitation that can be collected and stored.

Why is Freshwater Limited?

Most freshwater is not immediately accessible because it is stored in glaciers, ice caps and deep underground aquifers. The accessible surface water is limited and unevenly distributed.

7. Multipurpose River Projects

−

Meaning

Multipurpose river projects are projects designed to use river water for several purposes simultaneously, such as irrigation, electricity generation, flood control, water supply and navigation.

These projects generally involve the construction of dams and reservoirs across rivers.

Major Objectives

  • Irrigation
  • Hydroelectricity generation
  • Flood control
  • Water supply for domestic and industrial use
  • Inland navigation
  • Fish breeding
  • Recreation and tourism
A multipurpose project is intended to serve more than one objective through the planned use and management of river water.

8. Dams and Their Uses

−

What is a Dam?

A dam is a barrier constructed across a river or stream to control, store or divert water. The water stored behind a dam forms a reservoir.

Major Uses of Dams

  1. Irrigation: Stored water is supplied to agricultural fields.
  2. Electricity: Flowing water is used to generate hydroelectric power.
  3. Flood Control: Reservoirs can regulate the flow of water during periods of high discharge.
  4. Water Supply: Water is supplied to towns, cities and industries.
  5. Navigation: Water storage and regulation may support inland navigation.
  6. Recreation: Reservoirs may support tourism and recreational activities.
  7. Fisheries: Reservoirs can support fish production.

Types of Dams on the Basis of Height

  • Low dams: Relatively small structures.
  • Medium dams: Dams of intermediate height.
  • High dams: Large structures that can store substantial quantities of water.

Note: Dams can also be classified according to their construction material and structural design, such as gravity dams, arch dams and embankment dams.

−

India has a long history of water storage and management. Traditional societies constructed tanks, reservoirs, embankments and other structures to conserve water.

Ancient Water Management

  • Small dams and embankments were used to regulate water flow.
  • Traditional irrigation systems supported agriculture in different regions.

Modern River Valley Projects

After independence, India undertook several large river valley projects to support agricultural and industrial development.

10. Jawaharlal Nehru's View on Dams

−

Jawaharlal Nehru, India's first Prime Minister, supported large multipurpose river valley projects in the early years after independence.

He considered dams important for agricultural development, electricity generation and the modernisation of the country.

Famous Expression: Nehru described dams as the "temples of modern India".

Why Were Dams Called Temples of Modern India?

  • They were expected to support agricultural development.
  • They could generate electricity for industries and households.
  • They could help control floods.
  • They could provide water for domestic and industrial purposes.
  • They symbolised modern scientific and technological progress.

Over time, however, the social and environmental costs of large dams also became a subject of debate.

11. Benefits of Multipurpose River Projects

−

1. Agricultural Development

Canals and irrigation networks provide water to agricultural fields and help farmers cultivate crops in areas where rainfall is insufficient.

2. Hydroelectricity

Water stored in reservoirs can be released through turbines to generate electricity.

3. Flood Control

Reservoirs can store excess water and regulate river discharge, reducing flood risk under suitable operating conditions.

4. Drinking Water Supply

Stored water can be treated and supplied to households and urban areas.

5. Industrial Development

Industries require water for production, cooling and other processes. River valley projects can support industrial water requirements.

6. Inland Navigation

Regulated river flow may support transportation through navigable waterways.

7. Fisheries and Tourism

Reservoirs may support fish production, boating, recreation and tourism.

Multipurpose river projects can contribute to economic development when they are properly planned, managed and monitored.

12. Problems Associated with Large Dams

−

Although dams provide several benefits, large dam projects may also create serious social, economic and environmental problems.

1. Displacement of People

Large reservoirs may submerge villages, agricultural land and forests. People living in these areas may be forced to relocate.

2. Loss of Agricultural Land

Land used for farming may be submerged under reservoir water.

3. Destruction of Forests

Construction and reservoir formation may lead to the loss of forest areas and natural habitats.

4. Disturbance of Aquatic Ecosystems

Dams change the natural flow of rivers and can affect fish migration and aquatic biodiversity.

5. Sedimentation

Reservoirs may accumulate sediments carried by rivers. This can reduce storage capacity over time.

6. Unequal Distribution of Benefits

The benefits of irrigation, electricity and water supply may not be shared equally among all communities and regions.

7. Waterlogging and Salinisation

Excessive irrigation without proper drainage may cause waterlogging and the accumulation of salts in soil.

Large dam projects must be evaluated not only on the basis of their economic benefits but also on their social and environmental consequences.

13. Social and Environmental Impact of Dams

−

Social Impacts

  • Displacement of local communities.
  • Loss of houses and agricultural land.
  • Disruption of traditional livelihoods.
  • Loss of cultural and religious sites.
  • Problems related to rehabilitation and compensation.

Environmental Impacts

  • Submergence of forests and wildlife habitats.
  • Changes in river flow and sediment movement.
  • Effects on aquatic organisms.
  • Changes in local ecosystems.
  • Possible changes in water quality.

Impact on River Ecosystems

Rivers transport water, sediments and nutrients. They also provide habitats for numerous plants and animals. Dams alter the natural movement of water and sediments, which may affect river ecosystems.

Need for Sustainable Planning

  1. Conduct environmental impact assessments.
  2. Ensure fair rehabilitation and resettlement.
  3. Protect forests and wildlife habitats.
  4. Maintain environmental flows in rivers.
  5. Encourage public participation.
  6. Monitor the long-term performance of projects.

14. Opposition to Large Dams and Narmada Bachao Andolan

−

Why Did People Oppose Large Dams?

Opposition to large dams developed because of concerns about displacement, inadequate rehabilitation, environmental damage and unequal distribution of benefits.

Narmada Bachao Andolan

The Narmada Bachao Andolan is a social movement associated with concerns regarding large dam projects in the Narmada Valley, especially the Sardar Sarovar Project.

Major Concerns Raised

  • Displacement of people living in the Narmada Valley.
  • Submergence of villages and agricultural land.
  • Environmental damage and loss of forests.
  • Questions about rehabilitation and compensation.
  • Debate over who receives the benefits of the project.

Important Objective

The movement raised questions about development, environmental protection, the rights of affected communities and the need for fair rehabilitation.

Exam Point: Narmada Bachao Andolan is associated with opposition to large dam projects and concerns regarding displacement and environmental consequences.

15. Rainwater Harvesting

−

Meaning

Rainwater harvesting is the process of collecting and storing rainwater for future use instead of allowing it to flow away as surface runoff.

Objectives of Rainwater Harvesting

  • To conserve rainwater.
  • To recharge groundwater.
  • To reduce surface runoff.
  • To reduce dependence on external water supplies.
  • To improve water availability during dry periods.
  • To support sustainable water management.

Methods of Rainwater Harvesting

  1. Rooftop rainwater harvesting.
  2. Construction of recharge pits.
  3. Recharge wells and trenches.
  4. Storage tanks and underground tanks.
  5. Check dams and small barriers.
  6. Traditional ponds and tanks.

Advantages

  • Reduces pressure on groundwater.
  • Helps recharge aquifers.
  • Can provide water for domestic uses after suitable treatment.
  • Reduces urban flooding caused by runoff.
  • Provides a decentralised source of water.

Guls and Kuls

In the Western Himalayas, people developed diversion channels called guls or kuls to carry water from mountain streams to agricultural fields.

Khadins

Khadins are traditional water-harvesting structures in Rajasthan. They help collect surface runoff and conserve soil moisture for cultivation.

Johads

Johads are small earthen check dams or water-storage structures used to collect rainwater and improve groundwater recharge.

Ahar-Pyne System

The Ahar-Pyne system is a traditional water-management system found in Bihar. Ahars are reservoirs or embanked water bodies, while pynes are channels that carry water to agricultural fields.

Bamboo Drip Irrigation

In Meghalaya, bamboo pipes are traditionally used to transport water from streams to fields and plantations.

17. Rooftop Rainwater Harvesting

−

Meaning

Rooftop rainwater harvesting is a method of collecting rainwater from the roofs of buildings and storing it in tanks or directing it into groundwater recharge structures.

Main Components

  1. Catchment Area: The roof surface on which rainwater falls.
  2. Gutters: Channels that collect water from the roof.
  3. Downpipes: Pipes that carry water from the roof to the storage or filtration system.
  4. Filter: Removes leaves, dust and other impurities.
  5. Storage Tank: Stores collected rainwater.
  6. Recharge Structure: Allows water to enter the ground and replenish groundwater.

Working Process

  1. Rain falls on the rooftop.
  2. Gutters collect the rainwater.
  3. Downpipes carry water to a filter.
  4. Filtered water is stored in a tank or directed into a recharge pit.
  5. Stored water is used when required, after appropriate treatment.
Rainwater intended for drinking must be properly filtered, disinfected and tested for safety. Untreated rooftop runoff should not automatically be considered potable.

18. Rainwater Harvesting in Rajasthan

−

Rajasthan is a largely arid and semi-arid region. Many areas receive low and irregular rainfall. Therefore, traditional communities developed different methods of collecting and storing rainwater.

Tankas

Tankas are underground tanks used to store rainwater. They are traditionally found in parts of Rajasthan, especially in areas where water is scarce.

Features of Tankas

  • They are generally constructed underground.
  • They collect and store rainwater.
  • They may be connected to rooftops or courtyards through pipes and channels.
  • They help households store water for dry periods.

Khadins and Johads

Khadins and johads are traditional structures that help conserve runoff and improve local water availability.

Traditional rainwater harvesting systems demonstrate how communities adapted their water-management practices to local climatic conditions.

21. NCERT Exercise – Important Questions and Answers

−

Q1. Explain how water becomes a renewable resource.

Answer: Water becomes a renewable resource through the hydrological cycle. Water evaporates from oceans, rivers and other water bodies, condenses to form clouds and returns to the Earth's surface through precipitation. This continuous circulation replenishes water resources.

Q2. What is water scarcity and what are its main causes?

Answer: Water scarcity refers to the shortage of sufficient water to meet the needs of people and other users. Its main causes include population growth, urbanisation, industrialisation, intensive irrigation, over-exploitation of groundwater, water pollution and uneven distribution of rainfall.

Q3. Compare the advantages and disadvantages of multipurpose river projects.

Answer:

Advantages:

  • Provide irrigation facilities.
  • Generate hydroelectricity.
  • Help regulate floods.
  • Provide water for domestic and industrial purposes.
  • May support navigation, fisheries and tourism.

Disadvantages:

  • Displace people from their homes.
  • Submerge agricultural land and forests.
  • Disturb natural river ecosystems.
  • May cause waterlogging and soil salinity.
  • May result in unequal distribution of benefits.

Q4. What is rainwater harvesting? Explain its importance.

Answer: Rainwater harvesting is the process of collecting and storing rainwater for future use or groundwater recharge. It helps conserve water, replenish groundwater, reduce surface runoff and improve water availability during dry periods.

Q5. Describe traditional rainwater harvesting systems in India.

Answer: Different parts of India developed traditional water-harvesting methods according to local conditions. Guls and Kuls are found in the Western Himalayas; Khadins, Johads and Tankas are associated with Rajasthan; the Ahar-Pyne system is found in Bihar; and Bamboo Drip Irrigation is practised in Meghalaya.

Q6. Why are dams called multipurpose projects?

Answer: Dams are called multipurpose projects because the water stored in reservoirs can be used for several purposes, including irrigation, hydroelectricity generation, flood control, domestic water supply, industrial use, navigation, fisheries and recreation.

Q7. Why is water conservation necessary?

Answer: Water conservation is necessary because usable freshwater is limited and unevenly distributed. Increasing population, agricultural demand, industrialisation and pollution put pressure on water resources. Conservation helps protect ecosystems and ensures water availability for future generations.

Q8. Why did movements develop against large dams?

Answer: Movements developed because large dams can displace communities, submerge forests and agricultural land, disturb ecosystems and create problems related to rehabilitation and unequal distribution of benefits. Such movements demand fair treatment of affected people and sustainable development.
