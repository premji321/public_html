Chapter Introduction

Resources are essential for human survival and development. Their availability, distribution, utilisation and conservation determine the quality of human life.

This chapter explains the classification of resources, resource planning, sustainable development, land resources, land degradation, soil formation, major soil types of India and methods of soil conservation.

Chapter Learning Objectives: Understand resource classification, sustainable development, resource planning, land use, soil formation, soil types and conservation measures.
1. UNDERSTANDING RESOURCES

1.1 Meaning of Resources

Everything available in our environment which can be used to satisfy our needs, provided it is technologically accessible, economically feasible and culturally acceptable, can be termed as a resource.

Three Essential Conditions

  1. Technologically accessible.
  2. Economically feasible.
  3. Culturally acceptable.
Resources are not simply free gifts of nature. They are a function of human activities. Human beings transform materials available in the environment into useful resources.

1.2 Relationship Between Nature, Technology and Institutions

Human beings interact with nature through technology and create institutions to accelerate economic development.

1.3 Classification of Resources

Resources are classified on four major bases:

CLASSIFICATION OF RESOURCES
Origin
Biotic and Abiotic
Exhaustibility
Renewable and Non-renewable

Individual, Community, National, International

A. On the Basis of Origin

1. Biotic Resources

  • Obtained from the biosphere.
  • Have life or are derived from living organisms.
  • Examples: Human beings, flora, fauna, fisheries and livestock.

2. Abiotic Resources

  • Composed of non-living things.
  • Examples: Rocks, metals, minerals and water.

B. On the Basis of Exhaustibility

1. Renewable Resources

  • Can be renewed or replenished by physical, chemical or mechanical processes.
  • Examples: Solar energy, wind energy, water, forests and wildlife.

2. Non-Renewable Resources

  • Take millions of years to form.
  • Some can be recycled, such as metals.
  • Others cannot be recycled, such as fossil fuels.
  • Examples: Coal, petroleum and natural gas.

C. On the Basis of Ownership

TypeMeaningExamples
Individual ResourcesPrivately owned by individuals.House, plots, agricultural land.
Community ResourcesAccessible to all members of a community.Village ponds, public parks, playgrounds.
National ResourcesResources belonging to the nation.Forests, minerals, rivers, roads.
International ResourcesResources regulated by international institutions beyond national jurisdiction.Oceanic resources beyond the Exclusive Economic Zone.
Important: The oceanic resources beyond 200 nautical miles of the Exclusive Economic Zone belong to the open ocean and are regulated by international institutions.

D. On the Basis of Status of Development

1. Potential Resources

  • Found in a region but not fully utilised.
  • Examples: Solar and wind energy in Rajasthan and Gujarat.

2. Developed Resources

  • Resources whose quantity and quality have been surveyed.
  • Their utilisation has been determined according to available technology.

3. Stock

  • Materials in the environment which have the potential to satisfy human needs.
  • Appropriate technology to access or use them is not available.
  • Example: Water contains hydrogen and oxygen, which can be used as a source of energy, but the required technology is not available economically on a large scale in the textbook context.

4. Reserves

  • Subsets of stock.
  • Can be used to meet future requirements with existing technology.
  • Their use is being planned for the future.
  • Examples: Water stored in dams and forests preserved for future use.

2. DEVELOPMENT OF RESOURCES

2.1 Importance of Resources

  • Human survival.
  • Maintaining quality of life.
  • Agricultural development.
  • Industrial development.
  • Economic growth.
  • Transport and communication.
  • National development.

2.2 Problems Caused by Indiscriminate Utilisation

Indiscriminate utilisation of resources has created three major problems:

1. Depletion of Resources

Resources were depleted to satisfy the greed of a few individuals.

2. Unequal Distribution

Resources accumulated in the hands of a few people, dividing society into:

  • Haves – Rich people.
  • Have-nots – Poor people.

3. Environmental Problems

  • Global warming.
  • Ozone layer depletion.
  • Environmental pollution.
  • Land degradation.

2.3 Equitable Distribution of Resources

Equitable distribution means fair distribution of resources among people and regions.

  • Ensures sustained quality of life.
  • Promotes social justice.
  • Reduces economic inequality.
  • Supports global peace.

2.4 Need for Resource Planning

  • Resources are limited.
  • Distribution is unequal.
  • Some regions are rich in certain resources but deficient in others.
  • Overexploitation damages the environment.
  • Future generations also need resources.
Resource planning is essential for the sustainable existence of all forms of life.
3. SUSTAINABLE DEVELOPMENT

3.1 Meaning

Sustainable economic development means development should take place without damaging the environment, and development in the present should not compromise the needs of future generations.

3.2 Main Objectives

  1. Protect the environment.
  2. Use resources carefully.
  3. Maintain ecological balance.
  4. Ensure fair distribution of resources.
  5. Fulfil the needs of present generations.
  6. Preserve resources for future generations.

3.3 Rio de Janeiro Earth Summit, 1992

  • Year: 1992.
  • Place: Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.
  • More than 100 heads of states participated.
  • It was the first International Earth Summit.
  • Main purpose: Address environmental protection and socio-economic development at the global level.

Major Outcomes

  1. Declaration on Global Climatic Change and Biological Diversity.
  2. Endorsement of Global Forest Principles.
  3. Adoption of Agenda 21.

3.4 Agenda 21

Agenda 21 was a declaration signed by world leaders in 1992 at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED), held in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil.

Objectives

  • Achieve global sustainable development.
  • Combat environmental damage.
  • Reduce poverty.
  • Address disease.
  • Promote global cooperation.
  • Encourage shared responsibilities.
Important: One major objective of Agenda 21 was that every local government should prepare its own Local Agenda 21.

Availability of resources is necessary but not sufficient for regional development.

5. CONSERVATION OF RESOURCES

5.1 Meaning

Resource conservation means careful and planned use of resources to prevent their depletion and ensure availability for future generations.

5.2 Why Is Resource Conservation Necessary?

  • Resources are limited.
  • Overutilisation creates environmental problems.
  • Irrational consumption leads to resource depletion.
  • Future generations depend on existing resources.
  • Ecological balance must be maintained.

8. LAND DEGRADATION AND CONSERVATION MEASURES

8.1 Meaning of Land Degradation

Land degradation refers to the deterioration of the quality and productive capacity of land due to natural and human activities.

8.2 Major Causes of Land Degradation

A. Deforestation

  • Cutting down forests.
  • Mining-related deforestation.
  • Destruction of natural vegetation.

B. Overgrazing

Excessive grazing by livestock damages vegetation and exposes soil.

C. Mining and Quarrying

  • Mining removes vegetation and soil.
  • Abandoned mining sites leave deep scars.
  • Overburden creates additional land damage.

D. Over-Irrigation

  • Waterlogging.
  • Increased soil salinity.
  • Increased soil alkalinity.

E. Mineral Processing

Grinding limestone, calcite and soapstone produces dust. This dust settles on land and reduces water infiltration into soil.

F. Industrial Effluents

Untreated industrial waste and effluents contribute to land and water pollution.

9. SOIL AS A RESOURCE

9.1 Meaning and Importance of Soil

Soil is the most important renewable natural resource. It is the medium of plant growth and supports different types of living organisms on the earth.

  • Soil is a living system.
  • It takes millions of years to form a few centimetres of soil.
  • It consists of organic and inorganic materials.

9.2 Factors Responsible for Soil Formation

  1. Relief.
  2. Parent rock or bedrock.
  3. Climate.
  4. Vegetation and other forms of life.
  5. Time.

9.3 Natural Forces Responsible for Soil Formation

  • Changes in temperature.
  • Running water.
  • Wind.
  • Glaciers.
  • Activities of decomposers.
  • Chemical changes.
  • Organic changes.

9.4 Composition of Soil

10.2 Alluvial Soil

Distribution

  • Most widely spread and important soil in India.
  • Found throughout the northern plains.
  • Deposited by the Indus, Ganga and Brahmaputra river systems.
  • Also found in Rajasthan and Gujarat through a narrow corridor.
  • Found in eastern coastal plains, particularly the deltas of Mahanadi, Godavari, Krishna and Kaveri.

Composition

Alluvial soil consists of different proportions of sand, silt and clay.

Characteristics

  1. Generally fertile.
  2. Contains potash.
  3. Contains phosphoric acid.
  4. Contains lime.
  5. Suitable for intensive agriculture.
  6. Supports dense population.

Major Crops

  • Sugarcane.
  • Paddy.
  • Wheat.
  • Other cereals.
  • Pulses.

Types of Alluvial Soil

10.3 Black Soil

Other Names

  • Regur Soil.
  • Black Cotton Soil.

Distribution

  • Maharashtra.
  • Saurashtra.
  • Malwa.
  • Madhya Pradesh.
  • Chhattisgarh.
  • Parts of the Deccan Plateau.
  • Godavari and Krishna valleys.

Formation

  • Associated with the Deccan Trap region.
  • Formed from basaltic lava flows.
  • Parent rock material and climatic conditions are important factors.

Characteristics

  1. Black in colour.
  2. Extremely fine and clayey.
  3. High moisture-retaining capacity.
  4. Rich in calcium carbonate.
  5. Contains magnesium.
  6. Contains potash and lime.
  7. Generally poor in phosphoric content.
  8. Develops deep cracks during hot weather.
  9. Cracks help in proper aeration.
  10. Sticky when wet and difficult to work unless tilled at the appropriate time.

Major Crop

Cotton. Black soil is especially suitable for cotton cultivation because of its high moisture-retaining capacity.

10.4 Red and Yellow Soil

Distribution

  • Eastern and southern parts of the Deccan Plateau.
  • Odisha.
  • Chhattisgarh.
  • Southern parts of the middle Ganga Plain.
  • Piedmont zone of the Western Ghats.

Formation

  • Develops on crystalline igneous rocks.
  • Common in areas of low rainfall.

Characteristics

  • Red colour develops due to diffusion of iron in crystalline and metamorphic rocks.
  • Yellow colour appears when the soil occurs in hydrated form.

10.5 Laterite Soil

Origin of Name

The word Laterite is derived from the Latin word later, meaning brick.

Formation

  • Develops under tropical and subtropical climates.
  • Associated with alternate wet and dry seasons.
  • Formed due to intense leaching caused by heavy rainfall.

Distribution

  • Southern states.
  • Western Ghats region of Maharashtra.
  • Odisha.
  • Parts of West Bengal.
  • North-eastern regions.

Characteristics

  1. Generally deep to very deep.
  2. Acidic in nature.
  3. Usually deficient in plant nutrients.
  4. Prone to erosion and degradation.
  5. Humus content depends on vegetation and environment.

Agricultural Uses

  • Tea.
  • Coffee.
  • Cashew nuts.

10.6 Arid Soil

Distribution

Dry and desert regions, particularly western Rajasthan.

Characteristics

  1. Red to brown in colour.
  2. Sandy texture.
  3. Saline in nature.
  4. Low humus content.
  5. Low moisture content.
  6. High temperature.
  7. Faster evaporation.
  8. High calcium content in lower horizons.
  9. Kanker layers restrict water infiltration.

Agricultural Use

After proper irrigation, arid soils can become cultivable.

Example: Western Rajasthan.

10.7 Forest Soil

Distribution

  • Hilly and mountainous regions.
  • Areas with sufficient forest cover.

Characteristics

  1. Soil texture varies according to the mountain environment.
  2. Loamy and silty on valley sides.
  3. Coarse-grained on upper slopes.
  4. Snow-covered Himalayan areas experience denudation.
  5. Soil in these areas is acidic and has low humus content.
  6. Lower valleys, river terraces and alluvial fans contain fertile soil.

12. SOIL EROSION AND SOIL CONSERVATION

12.1 Meaning of Soil Erosion

Soil erosion is the denudation of the soil cover and its subsequent washing down.

Soil formation and soil erosion generally occur simultaneously. Normally, a balance exists between the two. Human activities and natural forces can disturb this balance.

12.2 Causes of Soil Erosion

Human Causes

  • Deforestation.
  • Overgrazing.
  • Construction.
  • Mining.
  • Defective farming methods.
  • Ploughing up and down the slope.

Natural Causes

  • Running water.
  • Wind.
  • Glaciers.

12.3 Types of Soil Erosion

A. Gully Erosion

  • Running water cuts through clayey soils.
  • It creates deep channels called gullies.
  • The land becomes unsuitable for cultivation.
  • Such land is called bad land.
  • In the Chambal basin, these lands are called ravines.

B. Sheet Erosion

  • Water flows as a sheet over large areas down a slope.
  • The upper layer of soil is washed away.

C. Wind Erosion

  • Wind blows away loose soil from flat or sloping land.
  • It commonly affects dry and arid regions.

12.4 Methods of Soil Conservation

1. Contour Ploughing

  • Ploughing along contour lines.
  • Slows down the flow of water.
  • Reduces soil erosion on slopes.

2. Terrace Cultivation

  • Steps are cut out on slopes.
  • Reduces the speed of flowing water.
  • Restricts soil erosion.
  • Common in the Western and Central Himalayas.

3. Strip Cropping

  • Large fields are divided into strips.
  • Grass strips are left between crops.
  • These strips reduce the force of wind.

4. Shelter Belts

  • Rows of trees are planted to create shelter.
  • Reduce wind speed.
  • Help stabilise sand dunes.
  • Have contributed to desert stabilisation in western India.

B. Short Answer Questions – About 30 Words

Q1. Name three states having black soil and the crop which is mainly grown in it.
Maharashtra, Madhya Pradesh and Chhattisgarh have extensive black soil areas. Cotton is the main crop grown in black soil because it has a high moisture-retaining capacity.
Q2. What type of soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast? Give three main features of this type of soil.
Alluvial soil is found in the river deltas of the eastern coast. Three features:
  1. It is generally very fertile.
  2. It contains potash, phosphoric acid and lime.
  3. It is suitable for crops such as paddy, wheat and sugarcane.
Q3. What steps can be taken to control soil erosion in hilly areas?
Soil erosion in hilly areas can be controlled through contour ploughing, terrace cultivation, afforestation and planting shelter belts. These methods reduce the speed of flowing water and wind.

C. Long Answer Questions – About 120 Words

Q1. Explain land-use pattern in India and why has the land under forest not increased much since 1960–61?
Land in India is used for several purposes, including forests, agriculture, grazing, buildings, roads, industries and other non-agricultural activities. Major land-use categories include:
  1. Forests.
  2. Barren and waste land.
  3. Land under non-agricultural uses.
  4. Permanent pastures and grazing land.
  5. Miscellaneous tree crops and groves.
  6. Culturable waste land.
  7. Fallow lands.
  8. Net sown area.
Land use is influenced by physical factors such as topography, climate and soil, and human factors such as population density, technology, culture and traditions. Forest area has not increased sufficiently because land is also required for agriculture, industries, settlements, transport and other developmental activities. The National Forest Policy, 1952, outlined a target of 33% forest cover for maintaining ecological balance.
Q2. How have technical and economic development led to more consumption of resources?
Technical and economic development have increased the consumption of resources in several ways.
  1. Modern technology has made it possible to extract resources on a large scale.
  2. Industrial development has increased demand for minerals, coal and petroleum.
  3. Agricultural development has increased the use of water, machinery and other resources.
  4. Urbanisation has increased demand for land, electricity, transport and construction materials.
  5. Economic growth has increased production and consumption.
However, indiscriminate utilisation of resources has also caused depletion, environmental pollution, global warming and land degradation. Therefore, resource planning, conservation and sustainable development are essential to ensure that resources remain available for future generations.

Activity 2: Conservation of Resources in School

  1. Avoid wastage of water.
  2. Switch off lights and fans when not needed.
  3. Reduce paper wastage.
  4. Plant trees.
  5. Maintain school gardens.
  6. Separate biodegradable and non-biodegradable waste.
  7. Encourage reuse and recycling.

Activity 3: What If Oil Supplies Get Exhausted?

  • Transport systems dependent on petroleum would be affected.
  • Industries using petroleum would face difficulties.
  • Agriculture using diesel-powered machinery would be affected.
  • Prices of goods and transportation could increase.
  • Countries would need to adopt alternative energy sources.
  • Solar, wind, hydroelectric and other energy sources would become more important.
