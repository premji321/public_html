1. Introduction (परिचय)

British rule in India was administered through a series of Governors-General and Viceroys. These officials represented the East India Company or the British Crown and played an important role in the political, administrative, economic and social transformation of India.

Two Major Periods

Company Rule: Governor-General of Bengal / India (1773–1858)
Crown Rule: Viceroys of India (1858–1947)

After independence, India continued as a Dominion until 26 January 1950, with a Governor-General as its constitutional head.

2. Difference Between Governor-General and Viceroy

Governor-GeneralViceroy
Initially represented the East India Company.Represented the British Crown.
Position developed under British Parliamentary Acts.Position established after the Revolt of 1857.
Administered British territories in India.Administered India on behalf of the British monarch.
First: Warren Hastings (1773)First: Lord Canning (1858)
Important: Lord Canning was the last Governor-General under Company rule and the first Viceroy under Crown rule.

PART 3: GOVERNOR-GENERALS AFTER INDEPENDENCE (1947–1950)

First Governor-General of Independent India

  • Served from 15 August 1947 to June 1948.
  • First Governor-General of independent India.
  • Oversaw the transfer of power and initial integration-related arrangements.

First and Only Indian Governor-General

  • Tenure: June 1948 – 26 January 1950.
  • Last Governor-General of independent India.
  • India became a Republic on 26 January 1950.
  • The office of Governor-General was replaced by the office of President.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India.
