1. Introduction (परिचय)

British rule in India was administered through a series of Governors-General and Viceroys. These officials represented the British Crown or East India Company and played an important role in the political, administrative, economic and social transformation of India.

Difference between Governor-General and Viceroy

Governor-General Viceroy
Initially represented the East India Company. Represented the British Crown.
Position developed under British Parliamentary Acts. Position established after the Revolt of 1857.
Administered territories under Company rule. Administered India on behalf of the British monarch.
First: Warren Hastings (1773). First: Lord Canning (1858).
Important: Lord Canning was the last Governor-General under Company rule and the first Viceroy under Crown rule.

PART 1: GOVERNOR-GENERALS OF BENGAL AND INDIA (1773–1858)

2. Warren Hastings (1773–1785) – First Governor-General of Bengal

Appointed under: Regulating Act, 1773

Headquarters: Calcutta

Major Events and Reforms

  1. Regulating Act (1773): Established the post of Governor-General of Bengal. Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General.
  2. Supreme Court of Calcutta (1774): First Chief Justice was Sir Elijah Impey.
  3. First Anglo-Maratha War (1775–1782): Ended with the Treaty of Salbai (1782).
  4. Second Anglo-Mysore War (1780–1784): Fought against Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan. Ended with Treaty of Mangalore (1784).
  5. Pitt's India Act (1784): Introduced dual control over the East India Company and created the Board of Control.
  6. Revenue Administration: Revenue farming was introduced in some regions and revenue administration was reorganised.
  7. Judicial Reforms: Established civil and criminal courts and promoted the use of Indian personal laws in certain judicial matters.
  8. Asiatic Society of Bengal (1784): Founded by Sir William Jones during Hastings' tenure.

Important Facts:

  • First Governor-General of Bengal – Warren Hastings.
  • First Chief Justice – Elijah Impey.
  • Impeachment proceedings began in 1788.
  • Hastings was acquitted in 1795.
3. Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793)

Known as: Father of Indian Civil Services (commonly associated).

Major Reforms

  • Permanent Settlement (1793): Introduced in Bengal, Bihar and Odisha. Zamindars were recognised as landowners for revenue purposes and land revenue was permanently fixed.
  • Cornwallis Code (1793): Reorganised civil and criminal courts and separated revenue administration from judicial functions.
  • Civil Services Reforms: Increased salaries and restricted private trade and corruption among Company officials. Higher posts were largely reserved for Europeans.
  • Third Anglo-Mysore War (1790–1792): Fought against Tipu Sultan and ended with Treaty of Seringapatam (1792).

Important Facts:

  • Permanent Settlement – 1793.
  • Cornwallis – Modern Civil Services.
  • Third Anglo-Mysore War ended in 1792.
4. Sir John Shore (1793–1798)
  • Followed the policy of non-intervention.
  • Charter Act renewed in 1793.
  • Battle of Kharda (1795): Marathas defeated the Nizam of Hyderabad.
  • Faced political instability in Awadh and Hyderabad.
Exam Fact: John Shore is associated with the policy of non-intervention.
5. Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)

Subsidiary Alliance (सहायक संधि)

  • Indian rulers had to maintain British troops.
  • They had to pay for maintenance of these troops.
  • British Residents were appointed in their courts.
  • Rulers could not conduct independent foreign relations.
  • They could not employ Europeans without British permission.

Major Events

  • Fourth Anglo-Mysore War (1799): Tipu Sultan was defeated and killed at Seringapatam.
  • Second Anglo-Maratha War (1803–1805): Treaties of Deogaon and Surji-Anjangaon (1803) were important.
  • Fort William College (1800): Established in Calcutta to train Company officials in Indian languages and administration.
Important Facts:
  • Subsidiary Alliance – Wellesley.
  • Death of Tipu Sultan – 1799.
  • Fort William College – 1800.
6. Lord Cornwallis (Second Tenure – 1805)
  • Returned to India in 1805.
  • His second tenure was very short.
  • Died at Ghazipur, Uttar Pradesh, in October 1805.
7. Sir George Barlow (1805–1807)
  • Served as acting Governor-General.
  • Followed non-intervention.
  • Vellore Mutiny occurred in 1806.
  • Vellore Mutiny was an early major military uprising against British authority.
8. Lord Minto I (1807–1813)
  • Treaty of Amritsar (1809): Signed between Maharaja Ranjit Singh and the British. Sutlej River became an important boundary between their spheres of influence.
  • Charter Act (1813): Ended Company's trade monopoly in India except tea and trade with China.
  • Allowed Christian missionaries to operate in India.
9. Lord Hastings (1813–1823)
  • Anglo-Nepal War (1814–1816): Ended with Treaty of Sugauli (1816).
  • Third Anglo-Maratha War (1817–1818): Ended the political power of the Peshwa.
  • Peshwa Bajirao II was defeated.
  • Pindari War (1817–1818): Pindari groups were suppressed.
  • Ryotwari System: Developed by Thomas Munro and Alexander Read.
  • Implemented in parts of Madras and Bombay Presidencies.
  • Restrictions on press were relaxed.
10. Lord Amherst (1823–1828)
  • First Anglo-Burmese War (1824–1826).
  • Treaty of Yandabo (1826).
  • Barrackpore Mutiny (1824).
  • British acquired Assam and other territories from Burma under the treaty.
11. Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835) – First Governor-General of India

Major Reforms

  • Abolition of Sati (1829): Prohibited in Bengal Presidency through Regulation XVII of 1829. Raja Ram Mohan Roy campaigned against Sati.
  • Suppression of Thuggee: William Sleeman played an important role.
  • English Education (1835): Macaulay's Minute on Education promoted English as a medium of higher education.
  • Charter Act of 1833: Governor-General of Bengal became Governor-General of India. Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India.
  • East India Company was deprived of its commercial activities.
  • Law Commission was provided for.
  • Promoted modern education and administrative centralisation.
Important Facts:
  • First Governor-General of India – Bentinck.
  • Abolition of Sati – 1829.
  • Charter Act – 1833.
  • English Education Policy – 1835.
12. Sir Charles Metcalfe (1835–1836)
  • Acting Governor-General.
  • Removed restrictive licensing regulations on the press in 1835.
  • Known as the Liberator of the Indian Press.
13. Lord Auckland (1836–1842)
  • First Anglo-Afghan War (1839–1842).
  • British attempted to establish Shah Shuja on the Afghan throne.
  • British forces suffered a disastrous retreat from Kabul in 1842.
14. Lord Ellenborough (1842–1844)
  • Oversaw the end of the First Anglo-Afghan War.
  • Annexation of Sindh in 1843 by Charles Napier.
  • Gwalior Campaign (1843).
  • Made a controversial declaration about the gates of Somnath.
15. Lord Hardinge I (1844–1848)
  • First Anglo-Sikh War (1845–1846).
  • Treaty of Lahore (1846).
  • Treaty of Bhairowal (1846).
  • British influence increased in Punjab.
16. Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856) – Doctrine of Lapse

Doctrine of Lapse (व्यपगत सिद्धांत)

Under this policy, the British refused to recognise adopted heirs as legitimate successors in certain princely states where the ruler died without a natural male heir.

Major Annexations

  • Punjab – 1849.
  • Lower Burma (Pegu) – 1852.
  • Berar – 1853.
  • Awadh – 1856.

Railway Development

  • First passenger train ran on 16 April 1853.
  • Route: Bombay (Bori Bunder) to Thane.
  • Distance: approximately 34 km.

Other Reforms

  • Telegraph lines developed.
  • Post Office Act, 1854 introduced a uniform postal system.
  • Public Works Department established in 1854.
  • Wood's Despatch (1854) – Magna Carta of English Education in India.
  • Universities of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras established in 1857.
17. Lord Canning (1856–1862) – First Viceroy of India
  • Revolt of 1857: Began at Meerut on 10 May 1857.
  • Government of India Act (1858): Ended East India Company rule and transferred administration to the British Crown.
  • Queen's Proclamation (1858): Announced on 1 November 1858 at Allahabad.
  • Indian Councils Act (1861): Introduced legislative councils with nominated Indian members.
  • Indian Penal Code: Came into operation in 1862.
  • Indigo Revolt (1859–1860): Peasants in Bengal protested against European indigo planters.
  • Income Tax: Introduced in 1860 under James Wilson.

PART 2: VICEROYS OF INDIA (1858–1947)

After the Government of India Act, 1858, the administration of India was transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown.

18. Lord Elgin I (1862–1863)
  • Second Viceroy of India.
  • Wahabi Movement continued.
  • High Courts at Calcutta, Bombay and Madras began functioning in 1862.
  • Died at Dharamshala in 1863.
19. Sir John Lawrence (1864–1869)
  • Bhutan War (1864–1865).
  • Treaty of Sinchula (1865).
  • Orissa famine occurred in 1866.
  • Railway and telegraph networks expanded.
  • Followed non-interference in Afghanistan.
20. Lord Mayo (1869–1872)

Father of Financial Decentralisation

  • Financial decentralisation introduced in 1870.
  • First attempt at all-India census in 1872.
  • First synchronous census was conducted in 1881.
  • Mayo College established at Ajmer in 1875.
  • Assassinated on 8 February 1872 at Port Blair by Sher Ali Afridi.
21. Lord Northbrook (1872–1876)
  • Bihar famine (1873–1874).
  • Kuka / Namdhari Movement continued in Punjab.
  • Prince of Wales visited India in 1875.
  • Resigned in 1876 over differences concerning Afghan policy.
22. Lord Lytton I (1876–1880)
  • Royal Titles Act (1876): Queen Victoria assumed the title Empress of India.
  • Delhi Durbar (1877): Queen Victoria proclaimed Kaisar-i-Hind.
  • Great Famine (1876–1878).
  • Vernacular Press Act (1878): Restricted Indian-language newspapers.
  • Arms Act (1878): Restricted possession of arms without licence.
  • Second Anglo-Afghan War (1878–1880).
  • Treaty of Gandamak (1879).
  • Reduced or abolished certain import duties, particularly on cotton goods.
23. Lord Ripon (1880–1884)

Father of Local Self-Government

  • Vernacular Press Act repealed in 1882.
  • Local Self-Government Resolution (1882).
  • Hunter Commission (1882) reviewed education.
  • First Factory Act (1881).
  • Ilbert Bill (1883).
  • First synchronous all-India census in 1881.
24. Lord Dufferin (1884–1888)
  • Indian National Congress (1885): Founded in Bombay on 28 December.
  • A.O. Hume associated with its establishment.
  • First President – W.C. Bonnerjee.
  • 72 delegates attended the first session.
  • Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885).
  • Upper Burma annexed in 1886.
  • Bengal Tenancy Act (1885).
25. Lord Lansdowne (1888–1894)
  • Indian Councils Act (1892).
  • Age of Consent Act (1891).
  • Durand Line (1893).
  • Second census of this period in 1891.
26. Lord Elgin II (1894–1899)
  • Great Famine (1896–1897).
  • Bubonic plague spread in Bombay and other regions.
  • Chapekar brothers assassinated W.C. Rand and Lt. Ayerst in Pune in 1897.
  • Frontier disturbances and military operations.
27. Lord Curzon (1899–1905)

Partition of Bengal (1905)

  • Announced on 19 July 1905.
  • Implemented on 16 October 1905.
  • Eastern Bengal and Assam had Dacca as capital.
  • Western Bengal remained centred at Calcutta.
  • Triggered Swadeshi and Boycott movements.

Other Reforms

  • Indian Universities Act (1904).
  • Ancient Monuments Preservation Act (1904).
  • ASI strengthened; John Marshall appointed Director-General in 1902.
  • Police Commission (1902–1903), headed by Andrew Fraser.
  • NWFP created in 1901.
  • Famine Commission (1901), under Sir Anthony MacDonnell.
  • Gold Exchange Standard formally adopted in 1899.
28. Lord Minto II (1905–1910)
  • Morley-Minto Reforms (1909): Indian Councils Act, 1909.
  • Introduced separate electorates for Muslims.
  • Muslim League established at Dacca in 1906.
  • Surat Split of Congress in 1907.
  • John Morley was Secretary of State for India.
29. Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916)
  • Capital shifted from Calcutta to Delhi in 1911.
  • Partition of Bengal annulled in 1911.
  • Bihar and Orissa became a separate province in 1912.
  • Delhi Durbar held in 1911.
  • Bomb attack on Lord Hardinge in 1912.
  • Ghadar Party founded in 1913 in North America.
  • First World War began in 1914.
30. Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)
  • Lucknow Pact (1916).
  • Home Rule Movement (1916).
  • Champaran Satyagraha (1917).
  • Ahmedabad Mill Strike (1918).
  • Kheda Satyagraha (1918).
  • Government of India Act (1919).
  • Dyarchy introduced in provinces.
  • Rowlatt Act (1919).
  • Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919).
  • Khilafat and Non-Cooperation Movement (1920).
31. Lord Reading (1921–1926)
  • Chauri Chaura Incident (1922).
  • Non-Cooperation Movement withdrawn by Gandhi.
  • Swaraj Party founded in 1923 by C.R. Das and Motilal Nehru.
  • Kakori Train Action (1925).
  • RSS founded in 1925 by K.B. Hedgewar.
  • Communist Party of India formally established in India in 1925 according to the commonly used historical account.
32. Lord Irwin (1926–1931)
  • Simon Commission appointed in 1927 and arrived in India in 1928.
  • Nehru Report (1928).
  • Lahore Session of Congress (1929).
  • Purna Swaraj declared as the goal.
  • Dandi March (12 March–6 April 1930).
  • First Round Table Conference (1930).
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931).
  • Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev executed on 23 March 1931.
33. Lord Willingdon (1931–1936)
  • Second Round Table Conference (1931).
  • Third Round Table Conference (1932).
  • Civil Disobedience Movement resumed in 1932.
  • Communal Award (1932).
  • Poona Pact (1932), between Gandhi and Dr. B.R. Ambedkar.
  • Government of India Act (1935).
  • RBI began operations on 1 April 1935.
34. Lord Linlithgow (1936–1943)

Longest-serving Viceroy of British India

  • Provincial Elections (1937).
  • Congress Ministries resigned in 1939.
  • August Offer (1940).
  • Individual Satyagraha (1940).
  • Vinoba Bhave was the first individual satyagrahi.
  • Cripps Mission (1942).
  • Quit India Movement (8 August 1942).
  • Gandhi gave the slogan "Do or Die".
  • Bengal Famine intensified in 1943 after his tenure ended.
35. Lord Wavell (1943–1947)
  • Bengal Famine (1943).
  • Wavell Plan (1945).
  • Simla Conference (1945).
  • INA Trials (1945–1946).
  • Royal Indian Navy Revolt (1946).
  • Cabinet Mission (March 1946).
  • Interim Government (2 September 1946).
  • Direct Action Day (16 August 1946).
36. Lord Mountbatten (1947) – Last Viceroy of British India
  • Viceroy from 24 March 1947 to 15 August 1947.
  • 3 June Plan (1947), also known as Mountbatten Plan.
  • Indian Independence Act (1947).
  • Created two independent dominions: India and Pakistan.
  • Pakistan became independent on 14 August 1947.
  • India became independent on 15 August 1947.
  • Mountbatten became the first Governor-General of independent India.

PART 3: GOVERNOR-GENERALS AFTER INDEPENDENCE (1947–1950)

37. Lord Mountbatten (1947–1948)
  • First Governor-General of independent India.
  • Served from 15 August 1947 to June 1948.
  • Oversaw the transfer of power and initial integration-related arrangements.
38. C. Rajagopalachari (1948–1950)
  • First and only Indian Governor-General of India.
  • Tenure: June 1948–26 January 1950.
  • Last Governor-General of independent India.
  • India became a Republic on 26 January 1950.
  • Dr. Rajendra Prasad became the first President of India.
