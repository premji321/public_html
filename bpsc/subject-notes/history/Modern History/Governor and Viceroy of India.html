1. Introduction

The Governor-General and Viceroy were the most important executive authorities during British rule in India. Their administration played a major role in the expansion, consolidation and control of British power in India.

Important Difference: The Governor-General was the chief executive authority under the East India Company and later under the British Crown. After the Government of India Act, 1858, the Governor-General also became known as the Viceroy, representing the British Crown in India.

Important Constitutional Developments

  • 1773: Regulating Act created the office of Governor-General of Bengal.
  • 1833: Charter Act created the office of Governor-General of India.
  • 1858: Government of India Act transferred power from the East India Company to the British Crown.
  • 1858: Governor-General became the Viceroy of India.
  • 1947: British rule ended and India became independent.

2. Governor-Generals of Bengal (1773–1833)

The Regulating Act of 1773 established the position of Governor-General of Bengal. Warren Hastings became the first person to hold this office.

1. Warren Hastings (1773–1785)
  • First Governor-General of Bengal.
  • Regulating Act of 1773 was implemented during his period.
  • Supreme Court was established at Calcutta in 1774.
  • Introduced reforms in revenue administration.
  • Established the Board of Revenue in Calcutta in 1781.
  • Supported the translation and study of Indian classical texts.
  • Asiatic Society of Bengal was founded by William Jones in 1784 during his administration.
  • First Anglo-Maratha War ended with the Treaty of Salbai in 1782.
  • Second Anglo-Mysore War ended with the Treaty of Mangalore in 1784.
  • Pitt's India Act was passed in 1784.
Exam Point: Warren Hastings was the first Governor-General of Bengal.
2. Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793)
  • Introduced the Permanent Settlement in Bengal in 1793.
  • Known as the Father of Civil Services in India.
  • Introduced judicial and administrative reforms.
  • Separated revenue administration from judicial administration.
  • Reformed the police system.
  • Third Anglo-Mysore War was fought during his administration.
  • Treaty of Seringapatam was signed in 1792.
Exam Point: Permanent Settlement was introduced in 1793 by Lord Cornwallis.
3. Sir John Shore (1793–1798)
  • Followed a policy of non-intervention in Indian states.
  • Charter Act of 1793 was passed during his tenure.
  • Battle of Kharda took place in 1795 between the Nizam and the Marathas.
4. Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)
  • Introduced the Subsidiary Alliance System.
  • Fourth Anglo-Mysore War took place in 1799.
  • Tipu Sultan was killed in the Battle of Seringapatam in 1799.
  • Second Anglo-Maratha War took place from 1803 to 1805.
  • Fort William College was established in Calcutta in 1800.
  • Expanded British political influence in India.
Exam Point: Subsidiary Alliance is associated with Lord Wellesley.
5. Lord Cornwallis (1805)
  • Returned to India for a second term.
  • His second tenure was very short.
  • He died at Ghazipur in October 1805.
6. Sir George Barlow (1805–1807)
  • Served as acting Governor-General.
  • Vellore Mutiny took place in 1806.
  • Followed a policy of retrenchment and economy.
7. Lord Minto I (1807–1813)
  • Treaty of Amritsar was signed with Maharaja Ranjit Singh in 1809.
  • Charter Act of 1813 was passed during his tenure.
  • Established diplomatic relations with several Indian and foreign powers.
8. Lord Hastings (1813–1823)
  • Anglo-Nepalese War took place from 1814 to 1816.
  • Treaty of Sugauli was signed in 1816.
  • Third Anglo-Maratha War took place from 1817 to 1818.
  • End of the Peshwa's political power in 1818.
  • Established British supremacy over most of India.
  • Ryotwari system developed in Madras under Thomas Munro.
9. Lord Amherst (1823–1828)
  • First Anglo-Burmese War took place from 1824 to 1826.
  • Treaty of Yandabo was signed in 1826.
  • Barrackpore Mutiny occurred in 1824.
  • Capture of Bharatpur took place in 1826.

3. Governor-Generals of India (1833–1858)

The Charter Act of 1833 changed the designation of Governor-General of Bengal to Governor-General of India. Lord William Bentinck became the first Governor-General of India.

10. Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835)
  • First Governor-General of India under the Charter Act of 1833.
  • Abolished Sati in 1829 with the support of Indian reformers such as Raja Ram Mohan Roy.
  • Suppressed Thuggee during his administration.
  • English Education Act was passed in 1835.
  • Promoted English education in India.
  • Medical education developed in Calcutta.
  • Introduced administrative and financial reforms.
Exam Point: Sati was abolished in 1829 by Lord William Bentinck.
11. Sir Charles Metcalfe (1835–1836)
  • Known as the Liberator of the Indian Press.
  • Removed several restrictions on the Indian press in 1835.
  • Served as acting Governor-General.
12. Lord Auckland (1836–1842)
  • First Anglo-Afghan War began in 1839.
  • Tripartite Treaty was signed in 1838.
  • Focused on British strategic interests in Afghanistan.
13. Lord Ellenborough (1842–1844)
  • First Anglo-Afghan War ended during his tenure.
  • Sindh was annexed in 1843.
  • Charles Napier played an important role in the conquest of Sindh.
  • Gwalior campaign took place in 1843.
14. Lord Hardinge I (1844–1848)
  • First Anglo-Sikh War took place from 1845 to 1846.
  • Treaty of Lahore was signed in 1846.
  • Treaty of Bhairowal was signed in 1846.
15. Lord Dalhousie (1848–1856)
  • Introduced the Doctrine of Lapse.
  • Annexed several Indian states under the Doctrine of Lapse.
  • Punjab was annexed in 1849.
  • Second Anglo-Sikh War took place from 1848 to 1849.
  • Lower Burma was annexed in 1852 after the Second Anglo-Burmese War.
  • First railway line ran between Bombay and Thane in 1853.
  • Telegraph service was introduced between Calcutta and other places.
  • Postal reforms were introduced in 1854.
  • Wood's Despatch was issued in 1854.
  • Public Works Department was developed.
  • Awadh was annexed in 1856 on the grounds of alleged misgovernance.
Exam Point: Doctrine of Lapse is associated with Lord Dalhousie.
16. Lord Canning (1856–1862)
  • Last Governor-General under the East India Company and first Viceroy of India.
  • Revolt of 1857 took place during his tenure.
  • Government of India Act was passed in 1858.
  • Power was transferred from the East India Company to the British Crown.
  • Indian Councils Act was passed in 1861.
  • Indian High Courts Act was passed in 1861.
  • Universities of Calcutta, Bombay and Madras were established in 1857.
  • Introduced the policy of clemency after the Revolt of 1857.
Exam Point: Lord Canning was the first Viceroy of India.

4. Viceroys of India (1858–1947)

After the Government of India Act, 1858, the administration of India was transferred to the British Crown. The Governor-General also became the representative of the British monarch and was called the Viceroy.

1. Lord Canning (1858–1862)
  • First Viceroy of India.
  • Queen Victoria's Proclamation was issued in 1858.
  • Indian Penal Code was enacted in 1860.
  • Indian Councils Act was passed in 1861.
  • High Courts were established under the Indian High Courts Act of 1861.
  • Income tax was introduced in 1860 under James Wilson.
2. Lord Elgin I (1862–1863)
  • Served as Viceroy for a short period.
  • He died in Dharamshala in 1863.
3. Sir John Lawrence (1864–1869)
  • Followed a policy of non-intervention in Afghanistan.
  • Bhutan War took place from 1864 to 1865.
  • Orissa famine occurred in 1866.
  • Established the Punjab University in 1882? No — Punjab University was established later, in 1882, during Lord Ripon's period.
4. Lord Mayo (1869–1872)
  • Introduced financial decentralisation.
  • Established the Department of Agriculture and Commerce.
  • First all-India census was initiated in 1872, though it was not synchronous.
  • First State Railway policy developments took place.
  • He was assassinated at Port Blair in 1872 by Sher Ali Afridi.
Exam Point: Lord Mayo was assassinated in 1872 at Port Blair.
5. Lord Northbrook (1872–1876)
  • During his tenure, the Prince of Wales visited India in 1875.
  • Resigned in 1876 due to differences over policy.
6. Lord Lytton (1876–1880)
  • Delhi Durbar was organised in 1877 to proclaim Queen Victoria as Empress of India.
  • Vernacular Press Act was passed in 1878.
  • Arms Act was passed in 1878.
  • Second Anglo-Afghan War began in 1878.
  • Great Famine of 1876–78 occurred during his tenure.
  • Maximum age for the Indian Civil Service examination was reduced.
Exam Point: Vernacular Press Act, 1878, was passed during Lord Lytton's tenure.
7. Lord Ripon (1880–1884)
  • Known as the Father of Local Self-Government in India.
  • Repealed the Vernacular Press Act in 1882.
  • Local Self-Government Resolution was introduced in 1882.
  • Hunter Commission on Education was appointed in 1882.
  • First Factory Act was passed in 1881.
  • Ilbert Bill controversy took place in 1883.
  • Regular decennial census was conducted in 1881.
  • Punjab University was established in 1882.
Exam Point: Lord Ripon is called the Father of Local Self-Government in India.
8. Lord Dufferin (1884–1888)
  • Indian National Congress was founded in 1885.
  • First session of Congress was held in Bombay.
  • W. C. Bonnerjee presided over the first Congress session.
  • Third Anglo-Burmese War took place in 1885.
  • Upper Burma was annexed in 1886.
9. Lord Lansdowne (1888–1894)
  • Indian Councils Act was passed in 1892.
  • Factory Act of 1891 was passed.
  • Age of Consent Act was passed in 1891.
  • Durand Line agreement was signed in 1893.
10. Lord Elgin II (1894–1899)
  • Plague epidemic affected India during his tenure.
  • Famine of 1896–97 occurred.
  • Revolutionary activities began to become more prominent.
11. Lord Curzon (1899–1905)
  • Partition of Bengal was announced in 1905 and implemented on 16 October 1905.
  • Indian Universities Act was passed in 1904.
  • Ancient Monuments Preservation Act was passed in 1904.
  • Archaeological Survey of India was strengthened.
  • Police Commission was appointed in 1902 under Andrew Fraser.
  • North-West Frontier Province was created in 1901.
  • Younghusband Expedition to Tibet took place in 1903–04.
  • Delhi Durbar was organised in 1903.
Exam Point: Partition of Bengal, 1905, was carried out by Lord Curzon.
12. Lord Minto II (1905–1910)
  • Swadeshi Movement continued during his tenure.
  • Muslim League was founded in 1906 at Dhaka.
  • Surat Split of Congress took place in 1907.
  • Morley-Minto Reforms were introduced through the Indian Councils Act, 1909.
  • Separate electorates for Muslims were introduced.
Exam Point: Separate electorates for Muslims were introduced by the Indian Councils Act, 1909.
13. Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916)
  • Partition of Bengal was annulled in 1911.
  • Capital of British India was shifted from Calcutta to Delhi in 1911.
  • Delhi Durbar was held in 1911.
  • King George V and Queen Mary visited India.
  • Komagata Maru incident occurred in 1914.
  • Ghadar Movement developed during this period.
  • First World War began in 1914.
14. Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)
  • Lucknow Pact was signed in 1916.
  • Home Rule Movement gained momentum.
  • Champaran Satyagraha took place in 1917.
  • Ahmedabad Mill Strike took place in 1918.
  • Kheda Satyagraha took place in 1918.
  • Rowlatt Act was passed in 1919.
  • Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place on 13 April 1919.
  • Government of India Act, 1919, introduced dyarchy in provinces.
  • Non-Cooperation Movement began in 1920.
  • Khilafat Movement developed during this period.
Exam Point: Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place during Lord Chelmsford's tenure.
15. Lord Reading (1921–1926)
  • Chauri Chaura incident took place in 1922.
  • Non-Cooperation Movement was withdrawn in 1922.
  • Swaraj Party was founded in 1923 by C. R. Das and Motilal Nehru.
  • Kakori Train Action took place in 1925.
  • Rash Behari Bose and other revolutionaries remained active during this period.
16. Lord Irwin (1926–1931)
  • Simon Commission arrived in India in 1928.
  • Lala Lajpat Rai was injured during a protest against the Simon Commission in 1928 and died later.
  • Nehru Report was prepared in 1928.
  • HSRA activities became prominent.
  • Central Legislative Assembly bomb incident took place in 1929.
  • Lahore Session of Congress was held in 1929 under Jawaharlal Nehru.
  • Purna Swaraj resolution was adopted in 1929.
  • Civil Disobedience Movement began in 1930.
  • Dandi March took place in 1930.
  • First Round Table Conference was held in 1930.
  • Gandhi-Irwin Pact was signed in 1931.
Exam Point: Dandi March and Gandhi-Irwin Pact took place during Lord Irwin's tenure.
17. Lord Willingdon (1931–1936)
  • Second Round Table Conference was held in 1931.
  • Third Round Table Conference was held in 1932.
  • Communal Award was announced in 1932 by Ramsay MacDonald.
  • Poona Pact was signed in 1932 between Mahatma Gandhi and B. R. Ambedkar.
  • Civil Disobedience Movement was eventually withdrawn.
  • Government of India Act was passed in 1935.
18. Lord Linlithgow (1936–1943)
  • Longest-serving Viceroy of British India.
  • Government of India Act, 1935, was implemented in 1937.
  • Provincial elections were held in 1937.
  • Congress ministries were formed in several provinces.
  • Second World War began in 1939.
  • India was declared at war without consulting Indian leaders in 1939.
  • Congress ministries resigned in 1939.
  • August Offer was announced in 1940.
  • Individual Satyagraha began in 1940.
  • Cripps Mission came to India in 1942.
  • Quit India Movement began in August 1942.
Exam Point: Lord Linlithgow was the longest-serving Viceroy of India.
19. Lord Wavell (1943–1947)
  • Bengal famine occurred in 1943.
  • Wavell Plan was proposed in 1945.
  • Simla Conference was held in 1945.
  • INA trials took place in 1945–46.
  • Royal Indian Navy Revolt took place in 1946.
  • Cabinet Mission came to India in 1946.
  • Interim Government was formed in September 1946.
  • Direct Action Day was observed on 16 August 1946.
20. Lord Mountbatten (March–August 1947)
  • Last Viceroy of British India.
  • Announced the 3 June Plan in 1947.
  • Indian Independence Act was passed in July 1947.
  • India became independent on 15 August 1947.
  • Partition of India took place in 1947.
  • Became the first Governor-General of independent India.
  • Served as Governor-General until June 1948.
Exam Point: Lord Mountbatten was the last Viceroy of British India.

5. Governor-Generals of Independent India (1947–1950)

After independence, India continued as a Dominion until 26 January 1950. During this period, India had two Governors-General.

1. Lord Mountbatten (1947–1948)
  • First Governor-General of independent India.
  • Served from August 1947 to June 1948.
  • Oversaw the initial transition after independence.
2. C. Rajagopalachari (1948–1950)
  • First and only Indian Governor-General of India.
  • Served from June 1948 to 26 January 1950.
  • Last Governor-General of India.
  • India became a Republic on 26 January 1950.
Exam Point: C. Rajagopalachari was the first Indian and the last Governor-General of India.
After 26 January 1950, the office of Governor-General was abolished. The President of India became the constitutional head of the Republic.

10. Historical Timeline

1773 – Regulating Act

Warren Hastings became the first Governor-General of Bengal.

1784 – Pitt's India Act

British government control over Company affairs increased.

1793 – Permanent Settlement

Introduced by Lord Cornwallis in Bengal.

1798 – Subsidiary Alliance

Introduced by Lord Wellesley.

1829 – Abolition of Sati

Implemented under William Bentinck.

1833 – Charter Act

Created the office of Governor-General of India.

1853 – First Railway

Railway ran between Bombay and Thane.

1857 – Revolt of 1857

A major uprising against British rule took place.

1858 – Government of India Act

British Crown took direct control of India.

1885 – Indian National Congress

Founded during Lord Dufferin's tenure.

1905 – Partition of Bengal

Announced and implemented under Lord Curzon.

1909 – Morley-Minto Reforms

Separate electorates for Muslims were introduced.

1911 – Capital Shift

Capital of British India shifted from Calcutta to Delhi.

1919 – Jallianwala Bagh

Massacre took place at Amritsar.

1930 – Dandi March

Mahatma Gandhi launched the Salt Satyagraha.

1935 – Government of India Act

Provided for provincial autonomy.

1942 – Quit India Movement

A major mass movement against British rule.

1947 – Indian Independence

British rule ended on 15 August 1947.

1950 – Republic of India

India became a Republic on 26 January 1950.
