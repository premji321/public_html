1. Introduction to the Digestive System

⌄

The digestive system is a group of organs responsible for breaking down complex food into simpler, soluble substances that can be absorbed and used by the body.

It provides nutrients for energy, growth, development and repair of body tissues.

Main Functions

  • Ingestion of food.
  • Mechanical and chemical digestion.
  • Absorption of nutrients.
  • Assimilation of nutrients by body cells.
  • Removal of undigested waste.
Definition: Digestion is the process of converting complex, insoluble food substances into simple, soluble substances that can be absorbed by the body.

2. Alimentary Canal

⌄

The alimentary canal is a continuous muscular tube extending from the mouth to the anus.

Parts of the Alimentary Canal

  1. Mouth or Buccal cavity
  2. Pharynx
  3. Oesophagus
  4. Stomach
  5. Small intestine
  6. Large intestine
  7. Rectum
  8. Anus

Accessory Digestive Organs

  • Salivary glands
  • Liver
  • Gallbladder
  • Pancreas
Exam Fact: The small intestine is the longest part of the alimentary canal.

3. Mouth or Buccal Cavity

⌄

The mouth is the first part of the digestive system. Digestion begins here.

Functions of the Mouth

  • Teeth break food into smaller pieces.
  • The tongue mixes food with saliva.
  • Saliva moistens food.
  • Salivary amylase begins starch digestion.
  • The tongue helps in swallowing.

Salivary Amylase

Salivary amylase, also known as ptyalin, breaks down starch into smaller carbohydrates, including maltose and dextrins.

Important: Mechanical digestion starts with chewing, while chemical digestion of starch begins with salivary amylase.

4. Types of Human Teeth

⌄

Human teeth help in cutting, tearing, crushing and grinding food.

Type Number in Adult Function
Incisors 8 Cutting food
Canines 4 Tearing food
Premolars 8 Crushing and grinding
Molars 12 Grinding food

Important Facts

  • Milk teeth: 20.
  • Permanent teeth: 32.
  • Hardest substance in the human body: Tooth enamel.

5. Salivary Glands

⌄

Salivary glands produce saliva, which helps in moistening food and beginning carbohydrate digestion.

Three Pairs of Major Salivary Glands

Gland Location
Parotid Near the ears
Submandibular Below the lower jaw
Sublingual Below the tongue

Composition of Saliva

  • Water
  • Mucus
  • Salivary amylase
  • Electrolytes
  • Other protective substances
Exam Fact: Ptyalin is another name for salivary amylase.

6. Pharynx and Oesophagus

⌄

Pharynx

  • It is a common passage for food and air.
  • It helps in swallowing.
  • It connects the mouth to the oesophagus for food passage.

Oesophagus or Food Pipe

  • It connects the pharynx to the stomach.
  • Food moves through it by peristalsis.
  • It does not perform significant chemical digestion.
Peristalsis: The rhythmic contraction and relaxation of muscles in the digestive tract that moves food forward.

7. Stomach

⌄

The stomach is a J-shaped muscular organ located in the upper-left part of the abdominal cavity.

Functions of the Stomach

  1. Temporarily stores food.
  2. Mixes food with gastric juice.
  3. Begins protein digestion.
  4. Converts food into a semi-liquid substance called chyme.

Gastric Juice

Substance Function
Hydrochloric Acid (HCl) Creates an acidic environment and helps kill many microbes.
Pepsin Breaks proteins into smaller peptides.
Gastric Lipase Helps digest some fats.
Mucus Protects the stomach lining.

Important Cells of Gastric Glands

  • Parietal cells: Secrete hydrochloric acid.
  • Chief cells: Secrete pepsinogen.
  • Mucous cells: Secrete protective mucus.
Exam Fact: Protein digestion begins in the stomach due to the action of pepsin.

8. Small Intestine

⌄

The small intestine is the longest part of the alimentary canal. It is approximately 6 metres long in an adult.

Three Parts of Small Intestine

Part Description
Duodenum First part; receives bile and pancreatic juice.
Jejunum Middle part; major site of nutrient absorption.
Ileum Final part; absorbs remaining nutrients, vitamin B12 and bile salts.

Main Functions

  • Completes most chemical digestion.
  • Absorbs digested nutrients.
  • Transfers nutrients into blood and lymph.
Important: Maximum absorption of digested food takes place in the small intestine.

9. Villi and Absorption

⌄

Villi are tiny finger-like projections present on the inner surface of the small intestine.

Functions of Villi

  • Increase the surface area for absorption.
  • Contain blood capillaries to absorb glucose and amino acids.
  • Contain lacteals to absorb most dietary fats.
Microvilli: Tiny projections on the surface of intestinal epithelial cells. They further increase the absorptive surface area.

10. Liver

⌄

The liver is the largest internal gland in the human body.

Location: Upper-right part of the abdominal cavity, below the diaphragm.

Major Functions

  1. Produces bile.
  2. Regulates blood glucose levels.
  3. Stores glycogen, vitamins and iron.
  4. Helps in detoxification.
  5. Metabolises carbohydrates, proteins and fats.
  6. Produces albumin and many blood clotting factors.
Exam Fact: The liver is the largest internal gland of the human body.

11. Bile and Gallbladder

⌄

Bile is a digestive fluid produced by the liver and stored and concentrated in the gallbladder.

Functions of Bile

  • Emulsifies fats into tiny droplets.
  • Helps create suitable conditions for pancreatic lipase.
  • Helps in the absorption of fats and fat-soluble vitamins.

Gallbladder

  • It is a small pear-shaped organ located beneath the liver.
  • It stores and concentrates bile.
  • It releases bile into the duodenum through the bile duct.
Important: Bile does not contain digestive enzymes. The liver produces bile, while the gallbladder stores it.

12. Pancreas

⌄

The pancreas is a mixed gland because it performs both exocrine and endocrine functions.

Exocrine Function

It produces pancreatic juice containing digestive enzymes.

Enzyme Function
Pancreatic Amylase Digests starch.
Trypsin and Other Proteases Digest proteins.
Pancreatic Lipase Digests fats.
Nucleases Digest nucleic acids.

Endocrine Function

  • Insulin: Lowers blood glucose.
  • Glucagon: Raises blood glucose.
Exam Fact: Insulin and glucagon are produced by the islets of Langerhans.

13. Large Intestine

⌄

The large intestine is approximately 1.5 metres long.

Main Parts

  1. Caecum
  2. Colon
  3. Rectum
  4. Anal canal

Parts of Colon

  • Ascending colon
  • Transverse colon
  • Descending colon
  • Sigmoid colon

Functions

  • Absorbs water and electrolytes.
  • Helps form faeces.
  • Temporarily stores faecal matter.
  • Contains gut microorganisms that contribute to vitamin production and other metabolic processes.

Appendix

The appendix is a small, finger-like pouch attached to the caecum. It contains lymphoid tissue and may have a role in gut immunity.

Appendicitis: Inflammation of the appendix is called appendicitis.

14. Rectum and Anus

⌄

Rectum

  • It is the final part of the large intestine.
  • It temporarily stores faeces.
  • It helps coordinate defecation.

Anus

  • It is the opening through which faeces are eliminated.
  • Anal sphincter muscles help control defecation.
Defecation: The process of removing faeces from the body.

15. Process of Digestion

⌄

Digestion takes place through five major steps.

  1. Ingestion: Taking food into the mouth.
  2. Digestion: Breaking complex food into simpler substances.
  3. Absorption: Movement of nutrients into blood or lymph.
  4. Assimilation: Utilisation of absorbed nutrients by body cells.
  5. Egestion: Removal of undigested food from the body.

Digestion of Different Nutrients

Nutrient Final Digestion Products
Carbohydrates Monosaccharides, such as glucose
Proteins Amino acids
Fats Fatty acids and monoglycerides

16. Digestive Enzymes and Their Functions

⌄

Digestive enzymes are biological catalysts that help break complex food molecules into simpler substances.

Enzyme Source Function
Salivary Amylase Salivary glands Starch digestion
Pepsin Stomach Protein digestion
Pancreatic Amylase Pancreas Starch digestion
Trypsin Pancreas Protein digestion
Pancreatic Lipase Pancreas Fat digestion
Maltase Small intestine Maltose into glucose
Sucrase Small intestine Sucrose into glucose and fructose
Lactase Small intestine Lactose into glucose and galactose
Peptidases Small intestine Peptides into amino acids
Nucleases Pancreas Digest nucleic acids
Remember: Amylase acts on carbohydrates, proteases act on proteins, and lipase acts on fats.

17. Hormones Involved in Digestion

⌄
Hormone Source Function
Gastrin Stomach Stimulates gastric acid secretion.
Secretin Small intestine Stimulates bicarbonate-rich pancreatic secretion.
Cholecystokinin (CCK) Small intestine Stimulates pancreatic enzyme secretion and gallbladder contraction.
GIP Small intestine Helps regulate insulin secretion and reduces gastric activity.

19. Common Digestive Disorders

⌄
Disorder Description
Acidity / Acid Reflux Stomach contents flow back into the oesophagus.
Peptic Ulcer Open sore in the stomach or duodenal lining.
Diarrhoea Frequent passage of loose or watery stools.
Constipation Difficulty passing stools.
Jaundice Yellowing of skin and eyes due to increased bilirubin.

21. Important Differences

⌄

Small Intestine vs Large Intestine

Small Intestine Large Intestine
Approximately 6 metres long. Approximately 1.5 metres long.
Narrower in diameter. Wider in diameter.
Most chemical digestion occurs here. Little to no enzymatic digestion by human enzymes.
Major site of nutrient absorption. Absorbs water and electrolytes.
Contains villi. Does not contain villi.

Digestion vs Absorption

Digestion Absorption
Breakdown of complex food. Movement of nutrients into blood or lymph.
Mechanical and chemical processes. Transport across intestinal lining.
