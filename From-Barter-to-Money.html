Chapter Overview

Money makes exchange easier. This chapter explains barter, its limitations, the functions of money, the evolution of coinage and paper currency, and modern digital payments.

1. Barter System

Definition: A system of exchanging goods or services directly for other goods or services without using money.
  • It was the earliest form of exchange.
  • Commodities used included cowrie shells, salt, tea, tobacco, cloth, cattle and seeds.

Traditional Forms of Money

  • Rai stones: Giant stone discs used on Yap Island in Micronesia.
  • Tajadero: Copper chopping-knife-shaped objects used in Central Mexico and parts of Central America.
  • Tevau: Red-feather coils used in the Solomon Islands.

2. Why Do We Need Money?

Definition: Money is a commonly accepted means of making and receiving payments in exchange for goods and services.
  • Makes buying and selling easier.
  • Reduces difficulties of direct exchange.
  • Helps compare prices and save purchasing power for future use.
  • Facilitates trade between people in different places.

3. Limitations of Barter

Double Coincidence of Wants

Definition: Both people have something the other wants and can exchange directly.

Example: A farmer wants shoes and a shoemaker wants wheat.

Common Measure of Value

Definition: A common unit for comparing the values of goods and services.

Without it, deciding how much wheat equals a pair of shoes is difficult.

Divisibility

Definition: The ability to divide an object into smaller portions.

An ox cannot conveniently be divided to exchange for a sweater.

Portability

Definition: The ability to carry an object from one place to another.

Carrying an ox or heavy bags of wheat is inconvenient.

Durability

Definition: The ability of an object to last without being damaged or spoiled.

Wheat may rot or be eaten by rats.

4. Traditional Barter Practices in India

Junbeel Mela

  • An annual three-day socio-cultural fair in Morigaon district, Assam.
  • Jun means moon and Beel means wetland in Assamese.
  • The tradition dates back to the 15th century. Chiefs of the Tiwa, Karbi, Khasi and Jaintia communities gathered to discuss political matters and maintain friendly relations.
  • People exchange local produce, fruits, vegetables, herbs, spices, handmade goods and food.
  • The fair begins with Agni Puja, a prayer for universal well-being.

Other Examples

  • Book exchange: Trading books with friends.
  • Old clothes for utensils: Vendors exchange household items for used clothes.
  • Local product exchange: Exchanging agricultural and locally produced goods.

5. Basic Functions of Money

Medium of Exchange

Definition: Something commonly accepted for purchasing goods and services.

Example: Paying for a notebook.

Store of Value

Definition: The ability to preserve purchasing power for future use.

Example: Saving money to buy medicines later.

Common Measure of Value

Definition: A common unit used to express and compare prices.

Example: A pen costs ₹10 and a notebook ₹40.

Standard of Deferred Payment

Definition: Money as a common measure for payments made in the future.

Example: Paying the remaining amount for a book later.

6. The Journey of Money

Commodity Money

Definition: Commodities used as a means of exchange.

Examples: cowrie shells, salt, cattle, seeds and cloth.

Metal Coinage

Definition: The system of using metal coins as money.
  • Ancient rulers issued coins; different kingdoms had their own coinage.
  • Coins were made from gold, silver, copper or their alloys.
  • Coins of powerful rulers sometimes circulated beyond their kingdoms and helped trade.

Coin Terms

  • Minting: Process of producing coins; a mint is a coin-manufacturing facility.
  • Alloy: Material made by combining elements, at least one of which is a metal.
  • Obverse: Side bearing the principal design.
  • Reverse: Opposite side of a coin.

Ancient Indian Coins

  • Coins used gold, silver, copper and alloys.
  • Some were called kārṣhāpaṇas or paṇas; the chapter refers to certain punched symbols as rūpas.
  • Coins depicted animals, trees, hills, rulers, queens and deities.
  • Chalukya coins: Some featured Varaha, an avatar of Vishnu, and a royal parasol.
  • Chola coins: Some silver coins displayed a tiger emblem.

Roman Coins in India

  • Roman gold coins were discovered at Pudukkottai in Tamil Nadu.
  • Finds in Kerala and Tamil Nadu provide evidence of southern India's maritime trade.
  • According to the textbook, scholars concluded that this trade was in India's favour.

Important Facts

  • One anna equalled 1/16 of a rupee.
  • The ₹ symbol was adopted in 2010.
  • It was designed by Udaya Kumar of IIT Bombay and combines elements of Devanagari 'र' and Roman 'R'.

7. Paper Money

Definition: Currency issued in the form of paper notes and used to make payments.
  • Carrying and storing many coins became inconvenient as trade expanded.
  • Paper money provided a more convenient alternative.
  • According to the textbook, paper money was first used in China and introduced in India in the late 18th century.
  • Coins are commonly used for smaller denominations; paper notes are also used for higher denominations.

Currency and Denomination

  • Currency: The system of money used in a country; India's currency is based on the rupee.
  • Denomination: The stated value of a coin or note. Examples include ₹1, ₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200 and ₹500.

Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

  • The RBI is India's central banking authority and controls currency issuance as described in the textbook.
  • Banknotes contain security features intended to prevent counterfeiting and misuse.

8. New Forms of Money

Digital Money: Money represented and transferred electronically rather than physically exchanged as coins or notes.
  • Enables electronic payments and transfers between bank accounts.
  • Reduces the need to carry cash for many transactions.

Digital Payment Methods

  • Debit card: Generally used to pay from a linked bank account.
  • Credit card: Lets eligible customers purchase using credit, subject to its terms.
  • Net banking: Banking transactions through the internet.
  • UPI: Unified Payments Interface, enabling electronic transfers between bank accounts.
  • QR Code: Quick Response Code, a machine-readable pattern used to store information and facilitate digital payments.

Advantages

  • Reduce the need to carry cash.
  • Make many transactions quick and convenient.
  • Enable electronic transfers between bank accounts.
