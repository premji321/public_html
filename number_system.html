Chapter 1: Number Systems – Comprehensive Notes

1. Introduction and Overview

Foundation: This chapter establishes the basis for understanding different types of numbers and their relationships.

Real Number System (R): The overarching system encompassing all numbers we'll study.

Goal: To understand the properties, representations, and operations involving various number systems.

2. Number Systems: Definitions and Properties

  • Natural Numbers (N): Counting numbers: 1, 2, 3, ...
    • Properties: Closed under addition and multiplication.
    • N = {1, 2, 3, 4, ...}
  • Whole Numbers (W): Natural numbers including zero: 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
    • Properties: Closed under addition and multiplication.
    • W = {0, 1, 2, 3, 4, ...}
  • Integers (Z or I): Whole numbers and their negative counterparts: ..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...
    • Properties: Closed under addition, subtraction, and multiplication.
    • Z = {..., -3, -2, -1, 0, 1, 2, 3, ...}
  • Rational Numbers (Q): Numbers expressible as p/q, where p and q are integers, q ≠ 0.
    • Properties: Closed under addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division (except by zero).
    • Decimal representation: Terminating or non-terminating recurring.
    • Q = {p/q : p, q ∈ Z, q ≠ 0}
  • Irrational Numbers (S): Numbers that cannot be expressed as p/q.
    • Properties: Decimal representation is non-terminating and non-recurring.
    • Examples: √2, √3, π, 0.1010010001...
  • Real Numbers (R): The union of rational and irrational numbers.
    • Every real number corresponds to a unique point on the number line.
    • R = Q ∪ S

3. Decimal Expansions

  • Terminating Decimals: Represent rational numbers.
    • Example: 1/4 = 0.25.
  • Non-Terminating Recurring Decimals: Represent rational numbers.
    • Example: 1/3 = 0.333... = 0.3̄.
  • Non-Terminating Non-Recurring Decimals: Represent irrational numbers.
    • Example: √2 = 1.41421356...
  • Converting Decimals to p/q Form: For recurring decimals:
    • Let x = the decimal.
    • Multiply x by 10, 100, etc., to shift the decimal point.
    • Subtract the original equation from the new equation to eliminate the recurring part.
    • Solve for x.

4. Representing Numbers on the Number Line

  • Rational Numbers: Divide the segment between integers into equal parts. Locate the point corresponding to the fraction.
  • Irrational Numbers: Use geometric constructions (e.g., Pythagorean theorem). Example: Locating √2 involves constructing a right-angled triangle with sides 1 and 1.

5. Finding Rational Numbers Between Two Rational Numbers

  • Mean Method: (a + b)/2 gives a rational number between a and b. Repeat the process to find more numbers.
  • Equivalent Fractions Method: Find equivalent fractions with a common denominator. Identify rational numbers between the numerators.

6. Rationalization of the Denominator

  • Purpose: To eliminate irrational numbers from the denominator.
  • Techniques:
    • Multiply by the conjugate: (a + √b) and (a - √b).
    • Multiply by the radical itself: √a * √a = a.
  • Example: 1/(√2 + 1) = (√2 - 1)/((√2 + 1)(√2 - 1)) = √2 - 1.

7. Laws of Exponents for Real Numbers

am × an = am+n

(am)n = amn

am / an = am-n

am × bm = (ab)m

a0 = 1

a-m = 1/am

ap/q = (a1/q)p = (ap)1/q

8. Problem-Solving Strategies

  • Identify the Type of Number: Determine whether a number is rational or irrational.
  • Apply Decimal Expansion Rules: Convert decimals to p/q form or vice versa.
  • Use Number Line Visualization: Draw number lines to understand the relative positions of numbers.
  • Practice Rationalization: Become proficient in rationalizing denominators.
  • Master Exponent Rules: Apply the laws of exponents to simplify expressions.
  • Verification: Always verify your answers, especially when dealing with irrational numbers.

Example Problem and Solution

Problem: Find three rational numbers between 1/4 and 1/2.

Solution: Convert to equivalent fractions with a common denominator: 1/4 = 2/8 and 1/2 = 4/8. Find rational numbers between 2/8 and 4/8: 3/8. To find more numbers multiply the numerator and denominator of both fractions by a number greater than the number of numbers you need to find. In this case we need to find 3 numbers, so we multiply by 4. 1/4 = 8/32 and 1/2 = 16/32. Therefore, three rational numbers are 9/32, 10/32 and 11/32.
